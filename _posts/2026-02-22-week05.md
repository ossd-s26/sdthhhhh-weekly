This week’s presentations were very interesting and creative. One group created a video that mimicked how the brain forgets information by gradually speeding it up, and it was honestly very funny and memorable. Many other groups built extensions that were either very useful in daily life or had very unique ideas. It was inspiring to see how different teams approached similar technical assignments in completely different ways.

Looking at our own highlight extension project, the biggest takeaway for me was communication. Dividing tasks clearly and cooperating efficiently made a big difference. When I did not understand something, asking my teammates helped me learn much faster. I realized that teamwork is not just about splitting work, but also about learning from each other’s strengths. Good communication directly improves efficiency.

From watching other groups’ presentations, I learned a lot about presentation skills. First, it is important to be confident and stand naturally on stage. Avoid unnecessary movements that distract the audience. Second, you need to be very familiar with your material. Reading directly from slides makes the presentation less engaging. The strongest presenters explained things in their own words and spoke smoothly, which made them sound more professional.

The three OSS conference videos we watched were also very helpful. Compared to student presentations, the speakers were more concise and focused more on the “why” behind their work. They told a story instead of just explaining technical details. Their slides were usually simple and clean, with less text. I noticed that strong speakers guide the audience through a clear structure and highlight the impact of their project, not just the features.

From all these presentations, I learned that technical work is only one part of a project. Being able to explain your idea clearly, confidently, and in a structured way is just as important. For my own style, I need to practice speaking more naturally without relying on reading slides, and I should focus more on explaining why the project matters, not just how it works.
