### Group Project Progress and Presentations

This week mainly focused on wrapping up our group work on the Frosty project and preparing for the final presentations. Frosty is an open-source mobile Twitch client built with Flutter that supports features like third-party emotes, background audio, and a more customizable user experience compared to the official app. Working on this project helped me better understand how real-world open source systems are organized.

<!--more-->

For my individual contributions, I worked on adaptive themed icons and explored implementing an audio focus setting. The adaptive icon feature allows the app icon to automatically adjust to system themes, which improves visual consistency on Android. The audio focus work was more logic-heavy, aiming to give users control over whether Frosty should interrupt other audio when a stream starts.

One of the biggest challenges I faced was not writing code, but figuring out where to write it. The codebase is large and unfamiliar, and it was difficult to confidently locate the correct files without risking breaking something else. I often had to trace existing implementations, read previous pull requests, and test small changes incrementally.

As a group, we collaborated on documentation improvements, including adding a Code of Conduct and contributing guidelines. We also worked together on the presentation, focusing on clearly explaining our process, challenges, and lessons learned.

Watching other groups’ presentations, I noticed that many teams faced similar problems, especially around inactive maintainers and difficulty finding suitable issues. The most effective presentations were those that focused on explaining their process rather than just showing results. This week helped me understand that open source work is not just about coding, but about navigating complex systems, communicating with others, and making meaningful contributions, even if they are small.
