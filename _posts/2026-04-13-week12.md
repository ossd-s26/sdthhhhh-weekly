### Industry and Open Source

This week we discussed how companies use and participate in open source projects. Before this class, I mostly thought of open source as something driven by individuals or communities. However, the discussion showed that many companies are deeply involved in open source, both as users and as contributors.

Many companies rely on open source software as part of their products or infrastructure. Using open source can reduce development time and cost, since companies do not need to build everything from scratch. At the same time, companies also contribute back to open source projects, either by fixing bugs, adding features, or maintaining projects that are important to their business.

<!--more-->

One interesting idea from the slides is that open source can be part of a company’s business model. For example, companies may provide free open source software, but make money through services such as support, hosting, or enterprise features. This helped me understand that open source is not only about sharing code, but also about creating sustainable ecosystems.

Another important point is that companies have different motivations for contributing to open source. Some want to improve tools they depend on, while others want to build reputation, attract developers, or influence the direction of a project.

### Personal Reflection

For my own experience, working on the Frosty project also reflects some of these ideas. Even though it is a community-driven project, the structure of contributions, issue tracking, and pull requests feels very similar to professional development workflows.

In the future, I think understanding how companies interact with open source will be very useful, especially when working in the industry. It shows that contributing to open source is not only about learning, but also about participating in a larger ecosystem.
